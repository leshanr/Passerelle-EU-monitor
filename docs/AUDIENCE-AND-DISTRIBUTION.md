# Audience and distribution

Distribution is not marketing bolted on at the end. For a publication like this
it is the harder half of the project, and it is the half a one-person operation
usually gets wrong.

---

## Who this is for

**Primary:** politically interested 18–30s — university students,
postgraduates, recent graduates, young professionals.

**Secondary:** people interested in Europe who do not follow EU politics
closely, and who currently get their European news either from national outlets
that cover the EU badly or not at all.

The reader this is written for: *knows what the European Commission is, could
not tell you what it did last week, and would like to.*

What that rules out: explaining what the EU is (patronising) and explaining a
trilogue without saying why it matters (bureaucratic). The line between them is
the whole craft.

---

## The honest lesson from last time

A student Substack launched without a committee behind it published two articles
and stopped. The failure was not the writing or the concept — it was that
everything from submissions to editing to promotion sat with one person, and
there was no mechanism that produced content when that person was busy.

**Passerelle is designed around that failure.** The monitoring system is the
mechanism: it produces the raw material whether or not anyone is paying
attention, so a busy fortnight costs you an edition rather than the project.
That is the single most important design decision here and it is worth saying
out loud in the deck.

The second lesson: distribution has to be built into the cycle as a named day
(day 14), not left as "and then share it".

## The university society network

This is the growth strategy, and it is the part with a genuine existing
advantage: a European Society co-founded at RHUL, membership of a
university-level European student network, an existing relationship with the
Goldsmiths EU society, and a working contact at the EU Delegation to the UK who
has already backed a student initiative once.

That is not a cold start. It is a network that already meets regularly and is
explicitly looking for things to do together.

### Target societies

| Type | Why they want this |
|---|---|
| EU / European societies | Content for their own feeds that they did not have to write |
| Politics and IR societies | Fortnightly explainer their members actually read |
| Student newspapers | A ready-made European politics column |
| Model UN societies | Background material for EU committees |
| Law societies | The court rulings beat is genuinely useful to them |

### The pitch, in one paragraph

> Passerelle is a fortnightly briefing on European politics written for people
> our age — 5–8 developments, ten minutes, no Brussels jargon. It runs on an
> automated monitoring system I built that watches the EU institutions
> continuously. I am looking for societies to collaborate with: co-branded
> explainers, guest contributions from your members, or just cross-posting when
> something lands in your area. It costs you nothing and gives your feed
> something substantive between events.

### Collaboration formats, easiest first

1. **Cross-posting.** They share a carousel. Zero effort for them. Start here.
2. **Co-branded explainer.** Their logo, your template, a topic they pick.
3. **Guest contribution.** A member writes 300 words on their country's angle.
   High value: it gives you national perspectives you cannot write yourself.
4. **Instagram takeover.** They run a day on your account or you on theirs.
5. **Interview.** With a society president, an academic, a Delegation contact.
6. **Joint event.** A panel or a briefing session. Highest effort, highest
   payoff, and the thing that turns a mailing list into a network.

### Sequencing

Do not approach thirty societies at once with nothing to show. The order that
works:

**Editions 1–3:** publish. Build an archive. Approach nobody.
**Editions 4–6:** approach your own network — the societies you already have a
relationship with — with three published editions as evidence.
**Editions 7–12:** widen to Politics and IR societies at other universities,
opening with the co-branded explainer offer.
**Beyond:** student newspapers, and the network-level relationships.

Three published editions is the minimum credible ask. One is a pitch; three is a
publication.

## Channel roles

| Channel | Job | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Substack** | Home of record. Depth. The archive you link to in applications. | Fortnightly |
| **Instagram** | Reach and accessibility. A second editorial format, not an advert. | 2–4 carousels per cycle |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional visibility. Lede plus link. | Per edition |
| **Society networks** | Distribution and collaboration. | Day 14 |

**Instagram is not a billboard for the Substack.** If a carousel only says "new
edition out now", it has wasted the slot. Each carousel should be worth reading
on its own, and a person who never opens the Substack should still learn
something. The Substack link is the last slide, not the point.

## What to measure

Vanity numbers will mislead you at this size. Four things worth tracking per
edition:

1. **Subscribers gained.** The only number that compounds.
2. **Open rate.** Below 35% means the subject lines are wrong, not the writing.
3. **Saves and shares on Instagram** — not likes. Saves mean it was useful.
4. **Society responses.** Replies to outreach, not follower counts.

Record them in the edition folder. Six editions in, you will be able to see
which beats actually travel, and that should change what you select on day 11.

## What this is worth professionally

Public affairs and policy job specs routinely ask for "experience of providing a
legislative and policy monitoring service". This is that service, run
independently, in public, with the code open and the archive linkable.

The line for an application:

> I run a fortnightly European politics briefing for an 18–30 audience, built on
> an automated monitoring pipeline of fifteen institutional sources that I
> designed and maintain. The code is public and so is every edition.

That sentence is only true once the schedule has fired and the archive has
issues in it. Until then: in progress.
