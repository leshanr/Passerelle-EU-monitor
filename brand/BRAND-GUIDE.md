# EU MONITOR — brand guide

**"Ink & Signal"**

The aesthetic target is *independent digital publication*, not *European
Commission social-media account*. European, political, modern, intelligent,
youthful — carried by typography and restraint rather than by putting the EU
flag on everything.

The reference points are Tortoise, The Overshoot and the better end of
independent newsletter design. The anti-reference is any institutional
infographic with twelve stars and a circle of holding hands.

---

## 1. The name

```
EU MONITOR
```

Always two words, always capitals in the logotype. Set in the display face,
tight tracking (−3%), with a **4px signal-blue rule** immediately beneath it at
the width of the word "MONITOR".

The rule is the whole logo. It is the one graphic element that appears on
everything — the Substack header, every carousel, the deck, the favicon — and
it is what makes a slide recognisable at thumbnail size.

Edition numbering is part of the mark, set in mono:

```
EU MONITOR
▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔
EDITION 07 · 27 AUG 2026
```

**Never:** stretch it, outline it, put it on a photo without a scrim, set it in
a serif, add a globe, add stars, or add a circle of dots meant to suggest
member states.

---

## 2. Logo concept

Three marks, one idea.

**Primary — the wordmark.** `EU MONITOR` + the rule. Used everywhere there is
room.

**Secondary — the monogram.** `EU` in the display face with the rule beneath it,
in a square. This is the Instagram avatar and the favicon. It has to work at
32px, which is why it is two letters and a line.

**Tertiary — the signal bar.** The 4px rule on its own, running the full width
of a slide. Used as a section divider and as the bottom edge of every carousel
slide. On its own it is not a logo, but after ten editions it reads as one.

The optional flourish: the rule can be drawn as a **flat line that spikes once**
— a monitoring trace with a single event in it. Use this sparingly (cover slides,
the Substack header) and never more than once per view. It is the visual
statement of the entire concept: mostly quiet, occasionally something matters.

## 3. Colour

| Role | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ink (ground) | `#0C0D10` | Near-black, faintly blue. Never pure `#000`. |
| Raised | `#14161B` | Cards and panels |
| Paper (text) | `#F2F1ED` | Warm off-white. Never pure `#fff` — it glares. |
| Dim | `#C6C6C1` | Secondary text |
| Muted | `#8A8F98` | Labels, metadata (5.98:1 on ink) |
| Rule | `#22242B` | Hairlines |
| **Signal** | **`#4F86FF`** | **The accent (5.72:1 on ink)** |
| Stale / warning | `#F0603C` | A dead source, a correction |

**The one rule about the accent:** one signal-blue element per view. A headline
word, or a number, or a button — not all three. The colour only works because it
is rare.

### Beat colours

Eight tags, always accompanied by the beat name in text. Colour identifies; the
label communicates.

| Beat | Hex |
|---|---|
| Power and democracy | `#F0603C` |
| War and security | `#EE4F89` |
| Tech and online life | `#4F86FF` |
| Climate and energy | `#1FA870` |
| Borders and moving around | `#BC8813` |
| Money and work | `#A06FF0` |
| Rights and courts | `#1F9DB0` |
| The UK and Europe | `#8A981F` |

### Charts

Fixed series order, never cycled: `#4F86FF` → `#F0603C` → `#1FA870` → `#BC8813`
→ `#A06FF0` → `#EE4F89`.

Slots 1–3 are validated for use *together in one chart* (all-pairs
colour-vision-deficiency separation). Beyond three series: facet into small
multiples, or fold the remainder into "Other". **Never generate a ninth hue.**

Every categorical value here was checked with a palette validator against the
dark surface — lightness band, chroma floor, CVD separation, contrast — rather
than chosen by eye.

### Light mode

There is one, and it is not an inversion. Ground `#F2F1ED`, text `#14161B`,
signal `#2F5AB8` (the dark-surface blue is too light on paper). Used for
printed one-pagers and for the deck's few light slides. Instagram and Substack
stay dark.

## 4. Typography

| Role | Face | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Space Grotesk** 700 | Caps or sentence case, −3% tracking, 0.94 leading |
| Body | **Inter** 400 / 500 | 1.55 leading, max 68 characters per line |
| Label | **JetBrains Mono** 500 | ALL CAPS, +18% tracking, always small |

All three are free, on Google Fonts, and available in Canva.

The identity lives in the **contrast between the display face and the mono
labels**. Big tight headline, small letterspaced mono label above it. That
pairing is doing most of the work; get it right and the rest is restraint.

```
MONITORING · 14 DAYS          ← mono, 11px, +18%, muted
The EU just changed           ← display, 64px, −3%, paper
who checks your age.
```

**Instagram type scale** (1080×1350):

| Element | Size | Face |
|---|---|---|
| Slide label | 24px | mono caps |
| Hook headline | 108px | display |
| Slide headline | 76px | display |
| Body | 40px | body |
| Caption / source | 26px | mono |

Nothing below 26px. It is read on a phone, at arm's length, while walking.

---

## 5. Recurring graphic elements

1. **The signal rule.** 4px, signal blue, bottom of every slide.
2. **Mono labels.** Every element gets a small letterspaced label above it.
   `WHAT CHANGED`, `SOURCE`, `EDITION 07`. This is the strongest single
   consistency device in the system.
3. **The slide counter.** `03/07` bottom-right of every carousel slide, mono,
   muted.
4. **Before → after.** Rendered as two stacked panels with an arrow between,
   the "after" panel outlined in signal blue. Used constantly, because "what
   changed" is the question most EU coverage skips.
5. **The flag chip.** A beat tag: mono caps, 1px border in the beat colour,
   4px radius. Never a filled pill — filled pills read as consumer app.
6. **The terminal block.** Monospace on `--ink-raised`, used whenever the
   monitoring system itself is being shown. It is what makes the automation
   visible in a visual system that is otherwise editorial.

## 6. Instagram layout

1080 × 1350 (4:5). Margin 80px all round. Bottom 120px reserved for the signal
rule, the slide counter and the source line.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────┐
│  EU MONITOR              03/07   │  ← mono, muted
│                                  │
│  WHAT CHANGED                    │  ← mono label, signal blue
│                                  │
│  Platforms used to               │  ← display, 76px
│  self-certify.                   │
│  Now they have                   │
│  to prove it.                    │
│                                  │
│  [ the visual — one per slide ]  │
│                                  │
│  Source: European Commission     │  ← mono, 26px, muted
│  ▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔  │  ← 4px signal rule
└──────────────────────────────────┘
```

**One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two visuals it is two slides. Seven
slides is the format; six is fine; nine is a Substack piece pretending to be a
carousel.

**Every slide carries one visual device** — map, timeline, bar chart, before/
after, comparison table, screenshot, or a single large number. A slide of pure
body text is the failure mode this format exists to avoid.

## 7. Substack

**Header:** ink ground, wordmark left, the strapline right in mono caps —
`EUROPEAN POLITICS. ACTUALLY WORTH KNOWING.` Signal rule beneath, full width.

**Edition header inside a post:** edition number and date in mono, the
issue's one-line summary in display, then a thin rule. Every issue looks the
same, which is what makes an archive look like a publication.

**In-post furniture:** beat chips above each item; `Why you should care` set as
a signal-blue mono label; the primary-source link always on its own line under
the item, in mono.

**Footer, every issue, unchanged:**

> EU Monitor is written by a person and found by a machine. The code that
> monitors EU sources is public: github.com/USERNAME/eu-monitor

---

## 8. What this must never look like

- A university society poster (gradients, drop shadows, three fonts).
- An EU institutional infographic (twelve stars, blue everywhere, stock photos
  of people shaking hands).
- A generic explainer account (rounded pastel cards, emoji as bullet points,
  Canva's default template).

The test: **would this look out of place next to a serious independent
publication?** If yes, cut whatever is causing it — it will almost always be a
decorative element that carries no information.
